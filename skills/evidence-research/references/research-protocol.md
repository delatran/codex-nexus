# Research protocol

Use the sections that address an actual evidence problem. An internal
source-to-claim record can be brief; create a separate ledger, matrix, or report
only when the requested research or its review needs one.

## Systematic coverage

For a literature review, comparative decision, or other claim about coverage,
define the population, time window, source families, inclusion rules, and
exclusions before screening outcomes. Record later boundary changes so an
exploratory revision is not presented as a prespecified review.

Start with relevant local records, user-provided seeds, and primary sources.
Vary search angles and follow useful references or citations. Screen the actual
source for method, relevance, completeness, date, and conflicts of interest.
Keep harvested, opened, included, and deeply analyzed counts separate if those
counts appear in the methodology or support a coverage claim.

Deduplicate versions and underlying evidence, not just URLs. Several articles
reporting the same experiment or dataset do not establish independent
corroboration. When research lines are split by region, period, population, or
hypothesis, use compatible claim definitions and inclusion rules before merging.

## Claim support and disagreement

For a material claim, retain the source identity or version and the passage,
page, table, record key, or other locator that supports it. Separate directly
reported findings, inference, assumption, unknowns, and exploratory hypotheses.
A title, search rank, word overlap, or the source author's confidence cannot
replace reading the support.

Check plausible alternative explanations where they could change the result:
selection effects, changed definitions, measurement limits, null findings,
stale versions, and omitted populations. Align population, time, and outcome
definitions before deciding whether two findings conflict. Preserve unresolved
disagreement without giving weak and strong evidence equal weight.

When a source is unavailable or only partly accessible, use only the content
actually inspected and explain any consequential gap. Do not infer an unseen
paper's findings from its title or a secondary citation.

## Causal, predictive, and economic claims

Match the conclusion to the design:

- For a causal claim, specify the effect being estimated, treatment or exposure,
  outcome, comparison, time window, confounders, selection risks, and
  identification limits. Without a credible comparison, report association or
  a hypothesis.
- For predictive performance, check whether the evaluation population and
  timing match the claimed use, whether tuning exposed the holdout, and whether
  uncertainty and external validation support generalization.
- For economic or cost claims, identify the denominator, currency, time horizon,
  included and excluded costs, baseline, and sensitivity to assumptions.
  Distinguish measured costs from a scenario estimate.

## Synthesis and completion

Answer from the supported claims and explain which findings drive the decision.
State uncertainty or trade-offs in proportion to their effect on the answer.
Complete the user's requested scope; do not use a fixed source quota as a proxy
for coverage. If targeted searches reach an evidence limit, report what is
missing and what observation could change the conclusion.

Use [claim and source packet](claim-source-packet.md) only when an auditable
record adds value. Its validator checks declared identity, freshness, and links,
not semantic support or truth.
